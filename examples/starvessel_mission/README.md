# A StarVessel round trip

Two reusable stages fly from a launch site at 26° N and both come home. The booster lifts the
stack, cuts off with its return propellant, flips, burns back towards the tower, falls tail-first
steering with its grid fins and brakes on thirteen engines and then three into the tower's arms.
The ship flies on to a 185 × 225 km orbit, releases a 100 t payload, coasts most of an orbit,
burns on the pass that comes back over the launch site, enters belly-first, falls belly-first
through the lower atmosphere, flips and lands on its legs beside the tower, about an hour and
three quarters after lift-off.

Both vehicles are 6-DOF free bodies on the turning Earth (`EquaForgeVC.Multibody`), with their
propellant feed (tanks, autogenous pressurisation, boil-off, header tanks), engine clusters with
turbopumps, electromechanical actuators on a battery bus, aerodynamics, a heat shield and a flight
computer each (this toolbox's `EquaForgeAerospace`). The vehicle data are **illustrative**:
rounded from public estimates for a fully reusable two-stage launcher, not any manufacturer's data.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `StarVesselMission.mo` | The package: the vehicles (`Booster`, `Ship`), their subsystems (`FeedSystem`, `ActuatorSet`, `AttitudeControl`, `ElectricBus`, `HeatShield`, `LandingLegs`, `Payload`), the flight computers and the eight models below |
| `wind.csv` | The wind against altitude (east and north), read by both vehicles' air data |
| `StarVesselMission_Analysis.ipynb` | The analysis worksheet (generated by the toolbox's `tools/make_starvessel_worksheet.py`) |

## The models

| Model | Span | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `AscentToOrbit` | 0–600 s | Lift-off, hot staging, the ship to orbit and its payload release, the booster back into the tower |
| `BoosterReturn` | 0–480 s | The booster alone (the ship as a mass): boostback steered by its impact point, the tail-first fall, the landing burn, the catch |
| `TowerCatch` | 405–480 s | The booster's last minute and the tower's mechanism: a sprung carriage, two arms on hinges that close, the booster's pins on the arms, bearing loads |
| `OrbitPlanning` | three orbits | The ship as a point in J2 gravity: elements, ground track, an optional Hohmann burn |
| `ShipEntryLanding` | 3900–6400 s | The ship from its parking orbit: deorbit burn, entry, belly-flop, flip and landing |
| `FullMission` | 0–6394 s | Everything in one run (13 s on a desktop), filtered outputs and a piecewise output grid; it stops 15 s after both vehicles are home |
| `AutopilotPlant` | 10 s | The landing ship's pitch on one engine, for `ef.linearize` |
| `Descent3DOF` | 20 s | The landing burn as a point mass, for `ef.optimal_control` |

```
          AscentToOrbit ──────────────────────────────┐
lift-off ─► hot staging ─┬─► ship: ascent ─► orbit ─► coast ─► deorbit ─► entry ─► belly-flop ─► flip ─► landed
                         │                    (OrbitPlanning)        (ShipEntryLanding)
                         └─► booster: flip ─► boostback ─► fall ─► landing burn ─► caught
                                  (BoosterReturn)                  (TowerCatch)
                                                FullMission: all of it
```

## Run it

In Studio, register the toolbox first (*Settings > Libraries > Add Folder*, the folder with
`package.mo` and `library.json`). Then open `StarVesselMission.FullMission` (*Examples →
Studies*) and simulate: altitude,
speed, orbit, ground track, mass and heat-shield plots open, and **Show in 3-D** plays the flight.
The 3-D view opens on the launch site (`LaunchComplex`: land, sea, deck, tower and arms, landing
pad) in the axes of its ground, so the site is level and the paths are drawn over the ground, and
the camera rides with the ship; choose `booster.look.hull` in the follow box to ride with the
booster, or *World axes* to see the flight in the model's inertial frame. Look inside `booster` and `ship`,
and further into `computer`, `feed`, `control` and `heatShield`: each diagram has a note on what it
does.

Open `StarVesselMission_Analysis.ipynb` and **Run All**, or in batch:

    equaforge run StarVesselMission_Analysis.ipynb -o result.ipynb

It runs in about a minute and a half and checks its results against theory as it goes: the
rocket equation and staging (SymPy), the ascent's losses and the feed's mass balance, vis-viva,
Kepler's period and the J2 node regression, a SciPy re-propagation of the orbit, a Hohmann raise,
the return opportunity on the first orbit, the booster's boostback shooting and the hover-slam,
Sutton–Graves entry heating, the fuel-optimal landing burn (`ef.optimal_control`), the autopilot's
margins (`ef.linearize`), a Monte Carlo of the catch and the whole mission's timeline.

From the command line, in this folder (`--lib` names the toolbox):

    equaforge simulate StarVesselMission.mo --lib ../.. --model StarVesselMission.FullMission -o mission.csv

## What the mission exercises

Free and capturable bodies, attachments and contacts (the launch mount, hot staging, the tower's
arms, the landing legs), mass properties that change with the propellant, gravity with J2, the
1976 standard atmosphere, air data with wind from a table, body and flap aerodynamics, grid fins,
propellant tanks with heat leak and relief, turbopumps on rotational shafts, chambers and nozzles,
engine clusters with engine-out, electromechanical actuators with their DC motors on a battery
bus, heat-shield thermal networks with radiation, orbital elements and ground tracks, an impact
point predictor, powered-descent guidance, mode sequencers, latches, hysteresis and timers, PI and
rate loops with variable limits, `reinit`, `terminate`, result filters and piecewise output grids.
